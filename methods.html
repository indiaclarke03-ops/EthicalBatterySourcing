What this is not

These are illustrative passports. They're not real product passports, and they make no statement about any company's compliance. Nothing here is legal, compliance or investment advice.

Evidence and confidence

Every value, supply-chain link and risk entry carries a source, an evidence tier, a provenance tag, a confidence score and a one-line reason.

Evidence tiers

TierMeaning
T1Primary source: regulation, regulator, filing, official company statement. A company claim is T1 as a statement, not as a measurement.
T2Credible secondary source or data provider: established press, industry analysts, licensed databases.
T3Vendor-published material, a single market-research house, or an unverified aggregation.

Confidence

ScoreWhen we use it
HighA primary (T1) source, or two independent credible secondary sources that agree.
MediumOne credible secondary source; a Sayari record that has passed our artefact check but has no public corroboration; sources that agree on substance but differ on figures; or a direct reading of regulation text applied to a case it doesn't name (the reason starts "Reading of T1 text:").
LowWeaker sources only; our own inference; an unresolved conflict between sources; or a database record that hasn't yet passed, or has failed, our artefact check.
UnknownNo usable source. These are drawn dashed and labelled "not traceable from public or licensed data".

Rules we apply the same way to every chemistry

  • Independent means independent. Several articles reporting one analyst's study count as one source.
  • We score the claim as worded. Evidence more than five years old supports "this was documented in 2016", not "this is happening now".
  • Company statements are statements. We report them as "the company states…". They're never treated as measurements.
  • "Not on a list" is dated. When we say a company isn't on a government list, we mean as of the date shown.
  • Database results need an extra check. Sayari records stay at Low until checked against three known patterns that can produce false links (below).
  • Same evidence, same score. A familiar chemistry (NMC, LFP) and an emerging one (Ni-H₂, carbon-O₂) are scored by the same rules.

Where sources disagree, we show every value side by side. We never average them.

Signals are not verdicts

Each risk entry has a signal type:

  • ● Documented harm: a credible investigation (NGO, journalist, academic field research) or a government finding describes harm at this place or in this activity.
  • ◆ Screening flag: a database or government list returned a match. Official government lists (the US UFLPA Entity List, the Section 1260H list) are named, with the government's stated reason. Database flags are shown only in aggregate unless a credible public investigation corroborates them. A screening flag is not an allegation.
  • ▲ Structural risk: the region or process carries documented risk, but we found no evidence about this specific site or company.
  • ○ No known evidence: we found nothing. Where the region or process carries structural risk, we show this as a gap, not as "clean".

Confidence and signal type are separate. A risk can be a High-confidence structural risk with no entity-level evidence at all.

Ethical-sourcing method

Every supply-chain node has an ethical-sourcing profile. Risk categories follow Annex X, point 2 of the EU Batteries Regulation (Reg. 2023/1542):

CodeCategory

For each risk we record the evidence and who documented it, the signal type, the EU due-diligence coverage, the confidence and its reason, and the assurance: what independent checking exists.

  • IRMA audit (Initiative for Responsible Mining Assurance);
  • RMI/RMAP list: a conformant smelter or refiner list exists for the material;
  • Other scheme (named);
  • Company self-assessment only;
  • No assurance known.

Rules

  • A screening flag is never upgraded to documented harm without a credible investigative or official source naming the entity.
  • Aggregation rule: supplier-level forced-labour flags from databases (Sayari) are published only as totals, unless a credible public investigation corroborates them.
  • Entities on official government lists (UFLPA Entity List, Section 1260H) are named, with the list, its date and the stated basis. They remain screening flags, not documented harm.
  • Harms are described from sources close to the people affected or from credible investigators, not only from company or database sources. No individual worker, community member or company director is named.
  • "No known evidence" is not "no risk".

What EU due diligence covers

The EU Batteries Regulation requires due diligence on four raw materials only: cobalt, natural graphite, lithium and nickel, plus their compounds used in active materials. The obligation applies from 18 August 2027 (as postponed by Reg. 2025/1561). Each risk entry says whether its material is covered, not covered, or unclear (for example, synthetic graphite). Coverage doesn't change our confidence in the evidence. It tells you whether the law would ask the question at all.

Sayari data

  • Sayari data is used with Sayari's permission.
  • Before scoring any Sayari finding above Low, we check it against three patterns that can produce misleading links:
    • longest-path traces;
    • custodian or registered-agent over-connection at depth;
    • stale registers after a company renames.
  • We check identifiers before matching names. For example, two unrelated companies on the UFLPA list both use "Jinchuan" in their names.

Examples found in this project

  • A battery maker's record under its former shell-company name listed itself as its own 99.8% shareholder: a stale register after a rename. We don't publish the flags attached to it.
  • Many trade-network flags rest on "Possibly the Same As" links between records that may or may not be the same company. Those flags are shown only as totals.
  • A US export-control list matched a flow-battery maker's name only because another organisation has a similar name. Name matches are never enough on their own.
  • A database flag called "MEU list contractors" turned out to mean "appears in government procurement records", not "on the Military End-User list". We read each flag's definition before using it.

Ethical commitments

  1. Passports are illustrative. No product is presented as compliant or non-compliant.
  2. Confidence is shown, never hidden. Unknown links are drawn as unknown; conflicting figures are shown side by side.
  3. Risk flags are screening signals, not allegations. Database flags are labelled as screening results, with the source, and published in aggregate unless corroborated.
  4. Affected people are not just data points. Nothing names individual workers or communities beyond what the source has already published.
  5. Data is used with permission. Sayari data is used with Sayari's permission. PitchBook figures appear only where India Clarke already verified them for her Electrum report, cited as "PitchBook, via Electrum report (Aug 2026)"; no new PitchBook data was pulled.
  6. Unverifiable information is left out. Where a claim couldn't be verified, it's recorded as a gap, not filled in.
