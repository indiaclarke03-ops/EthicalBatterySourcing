*Draft 2026-10-05 · Written for site visitors (ethical-sourcing practitioners, compliance teams, researchers) · Status: for India's review*

## 1. How confident are we?

Every value, supply-chain link and risk entry on this site carries a confidence score and a one-line reason.

| Score | When we use it |
|---|---|
| **High** | A primary source (regulation, regulator, government list, filing), or two independent credible secondary sources that agree. |
| **Medium** | One credible secondary source; a database record (Sayari) that has passed our artefact check but has no public corroboration; sources that agree on substance but differ on figures; or a direct reading of regulation text applied to a case it doesn't name. |
| **Low** | Weaker sources only; our own inference; an unresolved conflict between sources; or a database record that hasn't yet passed, or has failed, our artefact check. |
| **Unknown** | No usable source. These links are drawn dashed and labelled "not traceable from public or licensed data". |

**Rules we apply the same way to every chemistry:**
- **Independent means independent.** Several articles reporting one analyst's study count as one source.
- **We score the claim as worded.** Evidence more than five years old supports "this was documented in 2016", not "this is happening now".
- **Company statements are statements.** We report them as "the company states…". They're never treated as measurements.
- **"Not on a list" is dated.** When we say a company isn't on a government list, we mean as of the date shown.
- **Database results need an extra check.** Sayari records stay at Low until we've checked them against three known patterns that can produce false links. See §4.

Where sources disagree, we show every value side by side. We never average them.

## 2. Signals are not verdicts

Each risk entry has a **signal type**:

- **Documented harm:** a credible investigation (NGO, journalist, academic field research) or a government finding describes harm at this place or in this activity.
- **Screening flag:** a database or government list returned a match. Official government lists (e.g. the US UFLPA Entity List) are named, with the government's stated reason. Database flags are shown only in aggregate unless a credible public investigation corroborates them. **A screening flag is not an allegation.**
- **Structural risk:** the region or process carries documented risk, but we found no evidence about this specific site or company.
- **No known evidence:** we found nothing. Where the region or process carries structural risk, we show this as a **gap**, not as "clean".

Confidence and signal type are separate. A risk can be a High-confidence *structural* risk with no entity-level evidence at all.

## 3. What EU due diligence covers

The EU Batteries Regulation requires due diligence on four raw materials only: cobalt, natural graphite, lithium and nickel, plus their compounds used in active materials. Each risk entry says whether its material is **covered**, **not covered**, or **unclear** (e.g. synthetic graphite). Coverage doesn't change our confidence in the evidence. It tells you whether the law would ask the question at all.

## 4. Sayari data

- Sayari data is used with Sayari's permission.
- Before scoring any Sayari finding above Low, we check it against three patterns that can produce misleading links:
  - longest-path traces;
  - custodian or registered-agent over-connection at depth;
  - stale registers after a company renames.
- We also check identifiers before matching names. For example, two unrelated companies on the UFLPA list both use "Jinchuan" in their names.
- **Examples found in this project:**
  - A battery maker's record under its former shell-company name listed itself as its own 99.8% shareholder (a stale register after a rename). We don't publish the flags attached to it.
  - Many trade-network flags rest on "Possibly the Same As" links between records that may or may not be the same company. Those flags are shown only as totals.

## 6. What this is not

These are **illustrative** passports. They're not real product passports, and they make no statement about any company's compliance. Nothing here is legal, compliance or investment advice.
