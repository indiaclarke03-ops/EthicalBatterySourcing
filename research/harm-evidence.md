# Harm evidence by mineral (B-05b)

*As of 2026-10-05 · Researched with Claude Code (Tavily) · Status: for India's review*

## How to read this note

- One section per mineral or process. Each entry gives the harm, **who documented it**, where and when, its risk code (PLAN.md, Annex X), signal type, EU due-diligence coverage (from `ethical-sourcing.md`) and confidence.
- This note works at **mineral and region level**. Entity-level findings belong in the chemistry notes (B-09 – B-13), where each node is checked against these entries.
- **Wording rules** (SPECIFICATION §7): every harm is attributed ("Amnesty International documented…"). No individual worker or community member is named, even when the source names them.
- **Confidence** uses the same rubric for every mineral:
  - an NGO, investigative or academic source (T2) on its own → **Medium**;
  - two independent sources that agree, or a government finding (T1) → **High**.
- Primary reports are cited directly where found. Where only coverage of a report was reachable, the source is marked "via" and the entry is capped at Medium until the primary is read.

**The pattern behind the scorecard.** Every mineral outside the EU list that this project traces has documented harm: copper, manganese, phosphate (China), vanadium (China) and aluminium. Manganese has the strongest case: a US government child-labour listing, peer-reviewed pollution studies, investigative journalism and a Xinjiang labour-transfer finding.

## 9. Gaps from this pass

1. **Vanadium in South Africa and Russia:** no harm-specific source found.
2. **Sulphuric acid:** no harm source; treat as a dependency in B-07.
3. **Primary reports not yet read in full:** Global Witness (LI-4), Earthworks (NI-5), *Driving Force* (H12). Their entries stay at their current scores until a person reads the primary report.
4. **Graphite pollution (GR-1) and Chinese manganese pollution (MN-2) are 8–12 years old.** Look for 2023+ reporting before using them as current-state evidence. Otherwise label them "documented historically".
5. **Assurance:** none of the minerals outside the EU list has an assurance scheme (`ethical-sourcing.md` §4).
